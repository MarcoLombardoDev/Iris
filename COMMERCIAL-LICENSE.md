## 1. Do you actually need a commercial licence?

Most people do not. Iris ships **complete** under AGPL-3.0 — there is no paid tier, no feature held back, no usage cap, no licence key.

| What you want to do | Licence you need |
|---|---|
| Use Iris personally, or inside your company, for your own mailings | **AGPL-3.0 — free** |
| Install it on as many machines and for as many users as you like | **AGPL-3.0 — free** |
| Modify it for your own internal use | **AGPL-3.0 — free** |
| Fork it and publish your fork under AGPL-3.0 | **AGPL-3.0 — free** |
| Redistribute it, modified or not, keeping it AGPL-3.0 | **AGPL-3.0 — free** |
| Embed Iris (or part of its code) in a **closed-source product** | **Commercial** |
| Run a modified Iris as a **network service** without publishing your source | **Commercial** |
| **Resell** Iris, or ship it inside a product you sell, under your own terms | **Commercial** |
| Redistribute it under a licence **other than** AGPL-3.0 | **Commercial** |

The dividing line is simple: **AGPL-3.0 is free as long as the source stays open.** The commercial licence exists to remove exactly that obligation, and nothing else.

## 2. What the commercial licence grants

- The right to **integrate Iris, in whole or in part, into proprietary or closed-source products**, without the copyleft obligations of AGPL-3.0 §5 and §13.
- The right to **run a modified version as a network service** without publishing the corresponding source.
- The right to **distribute and sell** the resulting product under your own licence terms.
- Delivery of the **full source code** of the licensed version.
- The right to **modify the source** for your own product, with no obligation to contribute the changes back.

What it does **not** include: it is a licence, not a services contract. It carries no support commitment, no service level, no warranty, and no obligation on the author to develop specific features. The software is provided "AS IS", exactly as under AGPL-3.0.

## 4. Contributions and third-party code

Iris contains no third-party code beyond its declared Python dependencies, each of which keeps its own licence and is used unmodified. Contributions from other people are accepted only under the [Contributor License Agreement](CLA.md), which grants the Project Owner the right to license those contributions commercially. This is what makes it possible to offer a commercial licence at all — and it means a commercial licensee receives clean rights over the whole codebase.
